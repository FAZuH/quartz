## Cheatsheet: Time Series Components (Trend, Seasonal, Cyclical, Irregular)

> [!tip]
> If time series is a kite,
> - **Trend**: String that "pulls" or "leads" the kite to its "expected" path
> - **Seasonal**: The wind is always stronger at noon because the sun is out. (Predictable by time).
> - **Cyclical**: A "Storm System" is passing through. The wind howls for 3 days, then it’s calm for 10 days, then another storm lasts for 5 days. It is a long-term wave of energy that lingers, but there is no calendar for when the next storm starts.
> - **Irregular**: unpredictable deviations

## Definition: Weakly Stationary

Let $\gamma$ : Function of the lag $k$ and is independent of $t$.

A stochastic process $\{Y_t\}$ is **weakly stationary** 

If it satisfies three conditions:
1.  $E[Y_t] = \mu$   (constant mean)
2.  $\operatorname{Var}(Y_t) < \infty,\quad\forall t$   (finite variance)
3.  $\operatorname{Cov}(Y_t, Y_{t-k}) = \gamma_k$    (cov independent of $t$)

## Definition: Strictly Stationary

A [[2 Personal/rangkuman_202603011304#Definition Stochastic Process\|stochastic process]] $\{ Y_{t} \}$ is **strictly stationary**

If for any $n\in \mathbb{Z}^+$, any set of time points $\{ t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n} \}$, lag $k$ the joint cumulative distribution function of $(Y_{t_1}, Y_{t_2}, \dots, Y_{t_n})$ is identical to that of $(Y_{t_1+k}, Y_{t_2+k}, \dots, Y_{t_n+k})$

---

If $\{ Y_{t} \}$ is **strictly stationary** and $\operatorname{Var}(Y_{t})$ of  is finite for all $t$, then $\{ Y_{t} \}$ is also [[2 Personal/rangkuman_202603011304#Definition Weakly Stationary\|weakly stationary]]

## Some simple time series forecasting methods
### Naive method
Future prediction is based on the last observation.
$$F_{t+1} = X_t$$

### Averaging method
Forecasting is based on the average of all past observations.
$$F_{t+1} = \frac{1}{t} \sum_{i=1}^t X_i$$

### Smoothing methods

Methods to identify data patterns by smoothing local variation, generally using the average principle.

#### Formula: Single Moving Average (SMA)
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_t &= \frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=t-m+1}^t X_i \\
F_{t+1} &= S_t
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!tip]
> Average of the last $m$ data points. Suitable for stationary data patterns.

#### Formula: Double Moving Average (DMA)
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{1,t} &= \text{SMA of } X \\
S_{2,t} &= \text{SMA of } S_1 \\
F_{t+h} &= A_t + hB_t \\
A_t &= 2S_{1,t} - S_{2,t} \\
B_t &= \frac{2}{m-1}(S_{1,t} - S_{2,t})
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!tip]
> SMA process performed twice. Suitable for data with trends.
#### Formula: Single Exponential Smoothing (SES)
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_t &= \alpha X_t + (1-\alpha)S_{t-1} \\
F_{t+1} &= S_t
\end{aligned}
$$

Assigns exponentially decreasing weights to older data.

#### Formula: Double Exponential Smoothing (Holt's Linear Trend)
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_t &= \alpha X_t + (1-\alpha)(S_{t-1} + T_{t-1}) \\
T_t &= \gamma(S_t - S_{t-1}) + (1-\gamma)T_{t-1} \\
F_{t+h} &= S_t + hT_t
\end{aligned}
$$

Involves two smoothing parameters ($\alpha$ and $\gamma$) for level and trend.
#### Formula: Holt-Winter Seasonal Method 

Smoothing method for data with seasonal patterns with period length $p$.

**Additive**
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_t &= \alpha(X_t - M_{t-p}) + (1-\alpha)(S_{t-1} + T_{t-1}) \\
T_t &= \gamma(S_t - S_{t-1}) + (1-\gamma)T_{t-1} \\
M_t &= \delta(X_t - S_t) + (1-\delta)M_{t-p} \\
F_{t+h} &= S_t + hT_t + M_{t-p+h}
\end{aligned}
$$

Used when the seasonal component is constant relative to the level.

**Multiplicative**
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_t &= \alpha(X_t / M_{t-p}) + (1-\alpha)(S_{t-1} + T_{t-1}) \\
T_t &= \gamma(S_t - S_{t-1}) + (1-\gamma)T_{t-1} \\
M_t &= \delta(X_t / S_t) + (1-\delta)M_{t-p} \\
F_{t+h} &= (S_t + hT_t)M_{t-p+h}
\end{aligned}
$$

Used when seasonal variation changes along with the data level.
