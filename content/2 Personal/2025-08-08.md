## Distributions
Memorize pdf, mean, variance of:
- [[3 Reference/Continuous Distributions#Gamma distribution\|Gamma distribution]] : pdf, $\alpha \beta$, $\alpha \beta^2$
- [[3 Reference/Continuous Distributions#Chi-square distribution\|Chi-square distribution]] : $\Gamma(r/2, 2)$, $r$, $2r$
- [[3 Reference/Continuous Distributions#Normal distribution\|Normal distribution]] : pdf
- [[3 Reference/Discrete Distributions#Bernoulli distribution\|Bernoulli distribution]] : $p^x(1-p)^{1-x}$, $p$, $p(1-p)$
- [[3 Reference/Discrete Distributions#Binomial distribution\|Binomial distribution]] : $\binom nx p^x(1-p)^{n-x}$, $np$, $np(1-p)$
- [[3 Reference/Discrete Distributions#Poisson distribution\|Poisson distribution]] : pdf, $\lambda$, $\lambda$
$$
\begin{align}
 & \frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\beta^\alpha}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x/\beta} \\
 & \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{ 2\pi }}\exp\left[ -\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right] \\
 & \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!}
\end{align}
$$

## Distribution relationships
$$
\begin{align}
\bar{X} & = \frac{1}{n} \sum X_{i} \sim N\left( \mu, \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \right) \\
S^2 & = \frac{1}{n-1} \sum (X_{i} - \bar{X})^2
\end{align}
$$

## Definitions & theorems
### [[3 Reference/def-sufficient-statistic_202507170925\|Sufficient Statistic]]
$$ \frac{\prod_{i=1}^n f(x_{i},\theta)}{f_{Y_{1}}[u_{1}(\mathbf{x});\theta]} \quad \text{independent of }\theta$$


[[3 Reference/theorem-neyman-theorem_202508052229\|Neyman Theorem]]:
- $Y_{1}=u_{1}(\mathbf{x})$ [[3 Reference/Def-statistic\|Statistic]] for $\theta$
- $$\exists k_{1},k_{2}\ni \prod f(x_{i};\theta) = k_{1}[u_{1}(\mathbf{x});\theta] \cdot k_{2}(\mathbf{x})$$
- $k_{2}(\mathbf{x})$ independent of $\theta$

### [[3 Reference/def-regular-exponential-class_202507170942\|Regular Exponential Class]]
$$
f(x;\theta) = \begin{cases}
\exp[p(\theta)K(x) + H(x) + q(\theta)] & x\in \mathcal{S} \\
0 & \text{elsewhere}
\end{cases}
$$
- $\mathcal{S}$ independent of $\theta$
- $p(\theta)$ nontrivial
- $X$ continuous:
	- $K'(x)\neq 0$
	- $H(x)$ continuous
- $X$ discrete:
	- $K(x)$ nontrivial

## [[3 Reference/def-fisher-information_202507170919\|Fisher Information]]
$$
\begin{align}
I(\theta) & = E\left[ \left( \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} \ln f(X;\theta) \right)^2 \right] \\
 & = -E\left[ \frac{\partial^2}{\partial \theta^2}\ln f(X;\theta) \right] \\
I_{n}(\theta) & = nI(\theta)
\end{align}
$$

## [[3 Reference/def-rao-cramer-lower-bound_202507171109\|Rao-Cramer]] & [[3 Reference/def-efficient-estimator_202507170919\|Efficient Estimator]]
$$ \operatorname{Var}(Y) \geq \frac{[k'(\theta)]^2}{I_{n}(\theta)} $$

1. $Y$ is [[3 Reference/Def-unbiased-estimator\|Unbiased Estimator]] for $\theta$, i.e., $E(Y) = \theta$
2. $$ \operatorname{Var}(Y) = \frac{1}{nI(\theta)}$$

## Confidence interval
**Pivot variable**: Function of estimator of $\theta$, where dist and param of dist of the pivot variable is known

1. <u>Find estimator</u> for $\theta$, e.g., $\hat{\theta}=\bar{X}$ or $\hat{\theta}=S^2$
2. <u>Find pivot variable</u> $Z$, i.e., function of $\hat{\theta}$, where dist and param of dist of $Z$ is known
3. <u>Compute confidence interval</u> $$ 1-\alpha = P_{\mu}[\Phi<Z(\hat{\theta})<-\Phi] $$
4. Find <u>realization of confidence interval</u>

## Best test
1. Determine hypotheses: $H_{0} : \theta=\theta'$ vs $H_{1}: \theta=\theta''$
2. Construct $\lambda=\dfrac{L(\theta';\mathbf{x})}{L(\theta'';\mathbf{x})}$
3. Apply [[3 Reference/theorem-neyman-pearson-theorem_202508052132\|Neyman-Pearson Theorem]]. Best test has the form:
	- Reject $H_{0}$ if $\lambda \leq k$
	- Accept $H_{0}$ if $\lambda > k$
	Where $k>0$
4. Simplify test statistic:
	1. Write $\lambda\leq k$ in terms of $C=\{ \mathbf{x}:u(\mathbf{x};\theta',\theta'') \leq \text{(or }\geq)\ c \}$. $c$ usually constant obtained from $k$
	2. Use $\ln \lambda= \ln \dfrac{ L(\theta';\mathbf{x})}{ L(\theta'';\mathbf{x})}$
	or both
5. Get $c$ (critical value) such that $\alpha=P_{\theta'}(\mathbf{x}\in C)$

## UMPT
1. Determine hypotheses: $H_{0} : \theta=\theta'$ vs $H_{1}: \theta \in \Theta_{1}$
2. Construct $\lambda_{\theta_{1}}=\dfrac{L(\theta';\mathbf{x})}{L(\theta_{1};\mathbf{x})},\forall\theta_{1} \in \Theta_{1}$
3. Apply [[3 Reference/theorem-neyman-pearson-theorem_202508052132\|Neyman-Pearson Theorem]] for each $\theta_{1}$. Show critical region is the same:
   - Reject $H_{0}$ if $\lambda_{\theta_{1}} \leq k_{\theta_{1}}$
   - Accept $H_{0}$ if $\lambda_{\theta_{1}} > k_{\theta_{1}}$
   Where $k_{\theta_{1}}>0$
4. Simplify test statistic:
   1. Write $\lambda_{\theta_{1}}\leq k_{\theta_{1}}$ in terms of $C=\{ \mathbf{x}:u(\mathbf{x}) \leq \text{(or }\geq)\ c \}$. Show $C$ is independent of $\theta_{1}$
   2. Use $\ln \lambda_{\theta_{1}}= \ln \dfrac{ L(\theta';\mathbf{x})}{ L(\theta_{1};\mathbf{x})}$
   or both
5. Get $c$ (critical value) such that $\alpha=P_{\theta'}(\mathbf{x}\in C)$
