## Biodiversity

Biological diversity

> Totality of genes, species, and ecosystems of a region (CBD).
> 
> It is the variety within and between all species of plants, animals and micro-organisms and the ecosystems within which they live and interact (WWF).

### Genetic diversity

> Variety of <u>genes within a species</u>.

In one species, there are genes differences for: color of kernel, size, shape, disease resistance, pest resistance, yield, nutrient requirements, climactic conditions (habitat), response to stress environment (water, pH, temperature).

![[assets/image-70.png|450x254]]

### Species diversity

> Variety of <u>species within a region</u>.

![[assets/image-69.png|448x443]]

### Ecosystem diversity

> Variety of ecological complexes or biotic communities, including the interacting populations (all the plants, animals, microorganisms) within a particular area, and the abiotic factors that support them (habitat, climate, geological basis)

### Summary

Variety of

- **Genetic** diversity: Genes within a species
    
- **Species** diversity: Species within an ecosystem
    
- **Ecosystem** diversity: Interaction of biotic communities and abiotic factors
    

### Endemic

> A plants or animal that are <u>native and restricted to a certain place</u>.

### Biodiversity hotspot

> An area with a <u>high diversity of locally [[2 Personal/uas_202512091212#Endemic\|endemic]] species</u> (high concentration of endemic species in an area).


## Biogeography

> The study of the <u>distribution of living things</u> in space and time.
> 
> It analyzes patterns of biodiversity, distribution of species, and geological history.

### Plate Tectonics & Continental Drift

> The theory that Earth's outer shell is divided into several plates that glide over the mantle, causing the movement of continents.

- **Pangea:** A supercontinent that existed before 200 million years ago, which eventually broke apart into Laurasia and Gondwana.
- **Impact:** This movement isolated populations, leading to allopatric speciation and unique biodiversity distributions.
    

### Alfred Russel Wallace

> Known as the "Father of Biogeography".

- **Wallace Line:** An imaginary line separating the ecozones of Asia and Wallacea.
    - **West of the line:** Animals are related to Asian fauna (e.g., Tigers, Rhinos, Elephants).
    - **East of the line:** Animals are related to Australian fauna (e.g., Kangaroos, Marsupials).

![[assets/image-72.png]]

### Biogeography of Indonesia

Indonesia is divided into three main biogeographical units based on geological history (Plate Tectonics):

1. **Sunda Shelf (Paparan Sunda):** Previously attached to the Asian continent (Laurasia). Flora and fauna resemble Asian species.
2. **Sahul Shelf (Paparan Sahul):** Previously attached to the Australian continent (Gondwana). Flora and fauna resemble Australian species.
3. **Wallacea Region:** An isolated transition zone (Sulawesi, Maluku, Nusa Tenggara) containing high endemism.

## Threats to Biodiversity

> The current rate of species extinction is estimated to be 1000 times faster than the natural background rate, often referred to as the <u>Sixth Extinction</u>.

### Main Threats

- **Habitat Loss & Degradation:** Deforestation, fragmentation, and edge effects.
    
- **Overexploitation:** Hunting and excessive harvesting (e.g., whales, sharks, timber).
    
- **Invasive Species:** Introduction of non-native species that disrupt local ecosystems.
    
- **Climate Change:** Global warming affecting habitats and species survival.
    
- **Pollution:** Chemical contaminants affecting air, water, and soil.
    


## Conservation biology

> A branch of Biology that is concerned with the continuing loss of biodiversity and the development of practical solutions for protecting species, their habitats and ecosystems.

### Species conservation categories

- Extinct: Species that cannot be found anymore
    
- Endangered: Species that have a high probability of becoming extinct
    
- Vulnerable: Species that is about to be endangered in a short time period due to its declining population
    
- Rare: Species that has a low number of population
    
- Insufficiently known: Species that may be grouped to one of conservation categories, but there is not enough information to determine
    

### Biodiversity conservation

May be categorized into the following two:

#### In situ (on site) conservation

> Conservation of plant or animal or microorganism species in its natural ecosystem.

#### Ex situ (off site) conservation

> Conservation of an endangered species of plant or animal or microorganism outside its natural habitat.


## Biotechnology

> The use of <u>living organisms</u> to solve problems or make useful products for human welfare.

### History and Types

- **Ancient Biotechnology:** Domestication of animals and plants.
    
- **Traditional (Classical) Biotechnology:** Uses fermentation and selective breeding.
    
    - Examples: Tempe (_Rhizopus oryzae_), Cheese, Yoghurt, Soy sauce (_Aspergillus oryzae_).
        
- **Modern Biotechnology:** Involves the direct manipulation of genetic material (Genetic Engineering / Recombinant DNA Technology).
    

### Recombinant DNA Technology

> Production of a unique DNA molecule by joining together two or more DNA fragments not normally associated with each other.
>

> 
> Shutterstock

#### Basic Requirements

1. **Restriction Enzymes:** "Molecular scissors" used to cut DNA at specific sites.
    
2. **Ligase Enzymes:** "Molecular glue" used to join DNA fragments together.
    
3. **Vectors:** Vehicles to carry recombinant DNA into a host cell (e.g., **Plasmids** or Phages).
    
4. **Host System:** The organism where the recombinant DNA is replicated (e.g., _E. coli_, Yeast).
    

#### Key Techniques

- **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction):** Amplifies specific DNA sequences.
    
- **Gel Electrophoresis:** Separates DNA fragments based on size.
    
- **DNA Sequencing:** Determining the precise order of nucleotides in a DNA molecule.
    

### Applications of Biotechnology

#### Medical (Red Biotechnology)

- **Pharmaceuticals:** Production of human insulin using _E. coli_.
    
- **Vaccines:** Recombinant vaccines and "piggyback" vaccines.
    
- **Monoclonal Antibodies:** Antibodies capable of recognizing a specific antigen, used for diagnostics and therapy.
    
- **Stem Cells:** Using undifferentiated cells to regenerate tissues or organs.
    

#### Agricultural (Green Biotechnology)

- **GMO (Genetically Modified Organisms):** Organisms with inserted genes to acquire new traits.
    
    - **Golden Rice:** Rice fortified with beta-carotene (Vitamin A precursor) using genes from daffodils and bacteria.
        
    - **Bt Corn:** Corn containing a gene from _Bacillus thuringiensis_ to resist pests.
        

#### Forensic & Environmental

- **DNA Fingerprinting:** Identification based on unique DNA sequences, used in forensics and paternity testing.
    
- **Bioremediation:** Using microorganisms (e.g., _Rhodococcus sp._) to clean up pollutants and waste.
    

### Biosafety and Bioethics

> Biosafety: Efforts to ensure safety in handling and using biological organisms to prevent harm to humans and the environment.
> 
> Bioethics: The study of ethical decision-making associated with the use of living organisms.

**Concerns include:**

- Allergic reactions to GM foods.
    
- "Gene flow" from GM crops to wild relatives.
    
- Loss of natural biodiversity.
