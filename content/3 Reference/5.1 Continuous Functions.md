### Theorem

Let
- $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
- $f:A\to \mathbb{R}$
- $c \in A$

Then
- $f$ continuous at $c$
	- $\iff$ $\forall V_{\varepsilon}(f(c)),\exists V_{\delta}(c)\ni x\in V_{\delta}(c)\cap A\implies f(x)\in V_{\delta}(f(c))$
	- $\iff$ $f(x\in V_{\delta}(c)\cap A)\subseteq V_{\varepsilon}(f(c))$
	- $\iff$ $\forall(x_{n})\in A, (x_{n})\to c \implies (f(x_{n}))\to f(c)$
- $f$ discontinuous at $c$
	- $\iff$ $\exists (x_{n})\in A\ni(x_{n})\to c, (f(x_{n}))\not\to f(c)$

## 5.1.1 Definition: Continuous function at a point

> Let
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f:A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $c\in A$
> 
> If
> - $\forall \varepsilon>0,\exists \delta>0\ni |x-c|<\delta,\forall x\in A\implies|f(x)-f(c)|<\varepsilon$
> 
> Then
> - We say that **$f$ is continuous at $c$**
>
> Otherwise
> - We say that **$f$ is discontinuous at $c$**

> [!TIP] Resembles [4.1.4 Definition: Limit of a function](4.1%20Limits%20of%20Function.md#4.1.4%20Definition%20Limit%20of%20a%20function), but swap $L$ with $f(c)$

## 5.1.5 Definition: Continuous function on a set

> Let
> - $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f: A \to \mathbb{R}$
> - $B\subseteq A$
> 
> If
> - $f(b)$ continuous $\forall b\in B$
> 
> Then
> - We say that **$f$ continuous on $B$**

## 5.1.2 Theorem: Neighborhood definition of continuous function

> Let
> - $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f: A \to \mathbb{R}$
> - $c \in A$
> 
> Then the following statements are equivalent:
> - $f$ is continuous at $c$
> - $\forall V_{\varepsilon}(f(c)),\exists V_{\delta}(c)\ni x\in A\cap V_{\delta}(c)\implies f(x)\in V_{\delta}(f(c))$
> - $f(x\in A\cap V_{\delta}(c))\subseteq V_{\varepsilon}(f(c))$

## 5.1.3 Theorem: Sequential criterion

> Let
> - $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f: A \to \mathbb{R}$
> - $c \in A$
> 
> Then the following statements are equivalent:
> - $f$ is continuous at $c$
> - $\forall(x_{n})\in A, (x_{n})\to c \implies (f(x_{n}))\to f(c)$

> [!TIP] Resembles [4.1.8 Theorem: Sequential criterion](4.1%20Limits%20of%20Function.md#4.1.8%20Theorem%20Sequential%20criterion), but no $x_{n}\neq c\forall n\in N$ and swap $L$ with $f(c)$
>
> Notice that this implies $f(x)$ has to be defined at $x=c$

## 5.1.4 Theorem: Discontinuity criterion

> Let
> - $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f: A \to \mathbb{R}$
> - $c \in A$
> 
> Then the following statements are equivalent:
> - $f$ is discontinuous at $c$
> - $\exists (x_{n})\in A\ni(x_{n})\to c, (f(x_{n}))\not\to f(c)$
