## Definition

Let:
- $\text{Premises}$ : The starting assumptions of an argument
- $\text{Conclusion}$ : The proposition the argument aims to prove

Then an argument commits the **Begging the Question** fallacy if and only if:
$$
\text{Conclusion} \in \text{Premises}
$$

> [!TIP] Interpretation
> The argument assumes what it is trying to prove, making it logically circular.

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Bible's Authority
> **Scenario:** Someone argues the Bible is true because it says it's the word of God, and since it's the word of God, it must be true.
>
> **Why it fails:** The conclusion (the Bible is true) is used as a premise (it's the word of God). This circular reasoning provides no independent evidence.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: The Delusional Patient
> **Scenario:** A psychiatrist argues a patient is delusional because they claim aliens are communicating with them, and we know this is false because the patient is delusional.
>
> **Why it fails:** The diagnosis of delusion is based on the content of the belief, but the belief is deemed false based on the diagnosis of delusion. Independent evidence is needed.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: Murder is Wrong Because It's Illegal
> **Scenario:** "Murder is wrong because it's against the law, and the law prohibits murder because it's wrong."
>
> **Why it fails:** This explains nothing. The wrongness of murder and its illegality are being used to justify each other without providing an independent reason for either.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: Psychic Powers
> **Scenario:** "I know Jane has psychic powers because she predicted events correctly, and she predicted events correctly because she has psychic powers."
>
> **Why it fails:** The conclusion (psychic powers exist) is assumed in the premise. There's no explanation of *how* the predictions work or independent verification.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: Democracy is Best
> **Scenario:** "Democracy is the best form of government because the majority of people prefer it, and the majority prefers it because it's the best form of government."
>
> **Why it fails:** This circularly connects preference with quality without explaining what makes democracy effective or providing independent criteria for evaluation.
