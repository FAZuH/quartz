## Definition

Let:

- $C$ : A category
- $x$ : A counterexample that belongs to $C$
- $P$ : A property claimed to hold for all members of $C$

Then an argument commits the **No True Scotsman** fallacy if and only if it asserts:

$$
x \in C \text{ and } \neg P(x) \implies x \notin C \text{ (by redefining } C \text{ to exclude } x)
$$

> [!TIP] Interpretation
> The category is redefined ad hoc to exclude counterexamples, making the claim unfalsifiable.

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Real Christian
> **Scenario:** "No Christian would commit murder." When presented with a Christian murderer: "Well, no _true_ Christian would do that."
>
> **Why it fails:** The definition shifts to exclude counterexamples. By redefining "Christian" to only include good behavior, the claim becomes a tautology that can't be tested or falsified.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: The Rational Skeptic
> **Scenario:** "Skeptics never believe in conspiracy theories." When shown a skeptic who believes one: "Then they're not a _real_ skeptic."
>
> **Why it fails:** Rather than acknowledging that skeptics can have blind spots, the category is redefined to exclude any counterexample. This protects the claim from refutation.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: The Honest Business
> **Scenario:** "Our company never lies to customers." When evidence of lying emerges: "Those weren't _real_ company representatives."
>
> **Why it fails:** The category is narrowed to exclude embarrassing cases after the fact. This prevents the claim from ever being proven wrong, making it meaningless.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: The Feminist Definition
> **Scenario:** "Feminists support gender equality." When presented with a self-identified feminist who doesn't: "Then they're not a _real_ feminist."
>
> **Why it fails:** While definitions matter, simply excluding all counterexamples makes the claim unfalsifiable. Real movements contain diverse views and internal disagreements.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: The Scientific Method
> **Scenario:** "Science is always objective." When shown biased research: "That's not _real_ science, it's pseudoscience."
>
> **Why it fails:** While distinguishing science from pseudoscience is valid, this move protects the claim "science is always objective" from any counterexample by redefinition.
