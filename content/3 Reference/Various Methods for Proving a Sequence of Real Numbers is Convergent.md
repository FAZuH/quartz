Except for 3, all of these methods *require that we already know the value of the limit*, and we then verify that our suspicion is correct.

We can use the following definitions/theorems the conditions matches:

1. [[3 Reference/3.1 Sequences and Their Limits#3.1.3 Definition Converging and diverging (limit) of a sequence\|3.1.3]], [[3 Reference/3.1 Sequences and Their Limits#3.1.5 Theorem Sequence limit equivalency\|3.1.5]]. (*Often difficult*).
2. [[3 Reference/3.1 Sequences and Their Limits#3.1.10 Theorem Finding limit of a sequence\|3.1.10]]: Obtain by a **multiple of the terms in a sequence converging to $0$**
3. [[3 Reference/3.1 Sequences and Their Limits#3.1.9 Theorem Limit of a tail\|3.1.9]], [[3 Reference/3.2 Limit Theorems#3.2.3 Theorem Convergence and algebra operations\|3.2.3]], [[3 Reference/3.2 Limit Theorems#3.2.9 Theorem Limit of absolute sequences\|3.2.9]], [[3 Reference/3.2 Limit Theorems#3.2.10 Theorem Limit of square root of sequences\|3.2.10]]: Obtain **from other converging sequences**. 
4. [[3 Reference/3.2 Limit Theorems#3.2.7 Theorem Squeeze theorem\|3.2.7]]: Squeeze between **two sequences that has the same limit**.
5. [[3 Reference/3.2 Limit Theorems#3.2.11 Theorem Ratio test for convergence to zero\|3.2.11]]: **Ratio test**
6. [[3 Reference/3.3 Monotone Sequences#3.3.2 Theorem Monotone convergence\|3.3.2]]: Sequence is **monotone**.
