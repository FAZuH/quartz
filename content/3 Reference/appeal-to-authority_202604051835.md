## Definition

Let:
- $X$ : A person
- $P$ : A claim made by $X$
- $\text{Expertise}(X, P)$ : $X$ has relevant expertise regarding $P$

Then an argument commits the **Appeal to Authority** fallacy if and only if it asserts:
$$
X \text{ is an authority} \implies P \text{ is true} \quad \text{when } \neg \text{Expertise}(X, P)
$$

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: Celebrity Endorsement
> **Scenario:** A famous athlete claims that a particular energy drink improves athletic performance, and fans start buying it believing it will make them better athletes.
>
> **Why it fails:** The athlete's fame in sports doesn't make them an expert on nutrition or physiology. Their expertise is in athletics, not in evaluating supplements.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: Medical Advice from Actors
> **Scenario:** An actor who played a doctor on a TV show writes a book about health treatments, and readers trust the advice because they associate the actor with medical knowledge.
>
> **Why it fails:** Playing a doctor is not the same as being one. The actor has no medical training or expertise, regardless of how convincing their on-screen portrayal was.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: Tech CEO on Climate Science
> **Scenario:** A successful technology company CEO makes public statements about climate change policy, and people accept their views because of their business success.
>
> **Why it fails:** Expertise in technology and business management doesn't transfer to climatology. The CEO's authority in one domain doesn't make them an authority in another.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: Ancient Wisdom Fallacy
> **Scenario:** Someone argues that a medical treatment must be effective because it was described in texts from thousands of years ago by scholars of that time.
>
> **Why it fails:** Ancient scholars lacked modern scientific methods, controlled studies, and understanding of biology that we have today. Their authority in their own time doesn't equal expertise by modern standards.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: Noble Prize Winner Outside Their Field
> **Scenario:** A Nobel Prize winner in Literature makes claims about quantum physics, and people believe them because "they're a Nobel laureate."
>
> **Why it fails:** The Nobel Prize was awarded for literary achievement, not physics knowledge. Authority in literature doesn't confer authority in physics.
