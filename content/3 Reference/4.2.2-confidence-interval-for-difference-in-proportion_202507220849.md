## Proof: Confidence interval for difference in proportions
Let
- $X\sim \text{Binomial}(n_{1}, p_{1})$ : [[3 Reference/def-binomial-distribution_202509150822\|Binomial distribution]] with parameters $n_{1}$ and $p_{1}$
- $Y\sim \text{Binomial}(n_{2}, p_{2})$ : Binomial distribution with parameters $n_{2}$ and $p_{2}$
- $\Delta = p_{1} - p_{2}$ : Difference in proportions

Assume $X$ and $Y$ are independent.

Let
- $\hat{p}_{1} = \frac{X}{n_{1}}$ : Sample proportion for $X$
- $\hat{p}_{2} = \frac{Y}{n_{2}}$ : Sample proportion for $Y$
- $\hat{\Delta} = \hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2}$ : Sample difference in proportions

Then $E(\hat{p}_{1}) = p_{1}$ and $E(\hat{p}_{2}) = p_{2}$, so
$$E(\hat{\Delta}) = E(\hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2}) = E(\hat{p}_{1}) - E(\hat{p}_{2}) = p_{1} - p_{2} = \Delta$$

Thus $\hat{\Delta}$ is an [[3 Reference/Def-unbiased-estimator\|unbiased estimator]] of $\Delta$.

Additionally, $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{p}_{1}) = \frac{p_{1}(1-p_{1})}{n_{1}}$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{p}_{2}) = \frac{p_{2}(1-p_{2})}{n_{2}}$

By independence,
$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\Delta}) = \operatorname{Var}(\hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2}) = \operatorname{Var}(\hat{p}_{1}) + \operatorname{Var}(\hat{p}_{2}) = \frac{p_{1}(1-p_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{p_{2}(1-p_{2})}{n_{2}}$$

For sufficiently large $n_{1}$ and $n_{2}$, by the [[3 Reference/theorem-central-limit-theorem_202509190924\|Central Limit Theorem]], $\hat{p}_{1}$ and $\hat{p}_{2}$ are approximately [[3 Reference/3.4-the-normal-distribution_202507240800\|normally distributed]]:
$$\hat{p}_{1} \sim N\left(p_{1}, \frac{p_{1}(1-p_{1})}{n_{1}}\right) \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{p}_{2} \sim N\left(p_{2}, \frac{p_{2}(1-p_{2})}{n_{2}}\right)$$

Therefore,
$$\hat{\Delta} = \hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2} \sim N\left(p_{1} - p_{2}, \frac{p_{1}(1-p_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{p_{2}(1-p_{2})}{n_{2}}\right)$$

By standardization:
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\hat{\Delta} - \Delta}{\sqrt{\frac{p_{1}(1-p_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{p_{2}(1-p_{2})}{n_{2}}}} & \sim N(0, 1) \\
\frac{(\hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2}) - (p_{1} - p_{2})}{\sqrt{\frac{p_{1}(1-p_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{p_{2}(1-p_{2})}{n_{2}}}} & \sim N(0, 1)
\end{align}
$$

Since $p_{1}$ and $p_{2}$ are unknown, we estimate them using $\hat{p}_{1}$ and $\hat{p}_{2}$:
$$\frac{(\hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2}) - (p_{1} - p_{2})}{\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}}} \approx N(0, 1)$$

Thus the approximate $(1-\alpha)100\%$ [[3 Reference/def-confidence-interval_202507220823\|confidence interval]] for $\Delta$ is
$$
\begin{align}
1-\alpha & = P\left(-z_{\alpha/2} < \frac{\hat{\Delta} - \Delta}{\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}}} < z_{\alpha/2}\right) \\

& = P\left(-z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}} < \hat{\Delta} - \Delta < z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}}\right) \\

& = P\left(\hat{\Delta} - z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}} < \Delta < \hat{\Delta} + z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}}\right) \\

& = P\left((\hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2}) - z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}} < p_{1} - p_{2} < (\hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2}) + z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}}\right)
\end{align}
$$

From the last result, we can see that the following interval is an approximate $(1-\alpha)100\%$ confidence interval for $\Delta = p_{1} - p_{2}$
$$
\left(
(\hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2}) - z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}},\;\;
(\hat{p}_{1} - \hat{p}_{2}) + z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(1-\hat{p}_{1})}{n_{1}} + \frac{\hat{p}_{2}(1-\hat{p}_{2})}{n_{2}}}
\right)
$$
