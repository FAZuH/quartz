## 5.2.1 Theorem: Algebraic operations on continuous function in c

> Let
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f,g:A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $b\in \mathbb{R}$
> - $c\in A$
> - $f,g$ continuous at $c$
> 
> Then
> - $f+g, f-g, fg, bf$ continuous at $c$
> - $h:A\to \mathbb{R}$ continuous at $c$, $h(x)\neq0\forall x\in A\implies \frac{f}{h}$ continuous at $c$

## 5.2.2 Theorem: Algebraic operations on continuous function on A

> Let
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f,g$ continuous on $A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $b\in \mathbb{R}$
> 
> Then
> - $f+g, f-g, fg, bf$ continuous on $A$
> - $h:A\to \mathbb{R}$ continuous at $c$, $h(x)\neq0,\forall x\in A\implies \frac{f}{h}$ continuous on $A$

## 5.2.4 Theorem: Continuous absolute function

> Let
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f:A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $|f|$ defined by $|f|(x):=|f(x)|,\forall x\in A$
> 
> Then
> - $f$ continuous at $c\in A \implies |f|$ continuous at $c$
> - $f$ continuous on $A \implies |f|$ continuous on $A$

## 5.2.5 Theorem: Continuous square root function

> Let
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f:A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $f(x)\geq0,\forall x\in A$
> - $\sqrt{ f }$ defined by $(\sqrt{ f })(x):=\sqrt{ f(x) },\forall x\in A$
> 
> Then
> - $f$ continuous at $c\in A \implies \sqrt{ f }$ continuous at $c$
> - $f$ continuous on $A \implies \sqrt{ f }$ continuous on $A$

## 5.2.6 Theorem: Continuous composite at c

> Let
> - $A,B\in \mathbb{R}$
> - $f:A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $g:B\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $f(A)\subseteq \mathbb{B}$
> 
> If
> - $f$ continuous at $c\in A$
> - $g$ continuous at $b=f(c)\in B$
> 
> Then
> - $g \circ f:A\to \mathbb{R}$ continuous at $c$

## 5.2.7 Theorem: Continuous composite on A

> Let
> - $A,B\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f$ continuous on $A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $g$ continuous on $B\to \mathbb{R}$
> 
> If
> - $f(A)\subseteq B$
> 
> Then
> - $g\circ f:A\to \mathbb{R}$ continuous on $A$

> [!TIP] Intuition
> Result of $f(A)$ has to be the same as domain of $g$. i.e., $f(A)\subseteq B$.
> And note that $g$ is continuous on $B$.
