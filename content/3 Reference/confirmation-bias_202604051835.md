## Definition

Let:

- $P$ : A proposition or belief
- $E$ : Evidence
- $\text{Supports}(E, P)$ : Evidence $E$ supports proposition $P$

Then an argument exhibits **Confirmation Bias** if and only if:

$$
\text{Seeking only } E \text{ where } \text{Supports}(E, P) \text{ while ignoring } E' \text{ where } \neg \text{Supports}(E', P)
$$

> [!TIP] Interpretation
> This is the tendency to seek, interpret, and remember information that confirms existing beliefs.

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Horoscope Reader
> **Scenario:** Someone reads their horoscope and remembers all the predictions that seemed accurate while forgetting or rationalizing away the ones that were wrong.
>
> **Why it fails:** Selective memory creates the illusion of accuracy. By only counting "hits" and ignoring "misses," one can maintain belief in any system regardless of its actual validity.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: Political News Consumption
> **Scenario:** A person only watches news channels that align with their political views and dismisses opposing viewpoints as "biased" without examining them.
>
> **Why it fails:** This creates an echo chamber where existing beliefs are constantly reinforced and never challenged. Understanding issues requires engaging with diverse perspectives.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: Medical Self-Diagnosis
> **Scenario:** Someone searches online for symptoms and focuses only on information that confirms their feared diagnosis while ignoring evidence of more likely, benign causes.
>
> **Why it fails:** The anxiety-driven search leads to confirmation of worst fears rather than balanced evaluation. This can cause unnecessary stress and delay proper medical consultation.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: The Gambler's Pattern Recognition
> **Scenario:** A gambler believes they have a "system" because they remember their big wins vividly but downplay or forget their many losses.
>
> **Why it fails:** Selective memory of wins creates false confidence in a nonexistent pattern. The losses are just as real but psychologically minimized.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: Scientific Experimenter Bias
> **Scenario:** A researcher unconsciously interprets ambiguous data in favor of their hypothesis and discounts results that contradict their expected outcome.
>
> **Why it fails:** Science requires objective evaluation of all evidence. Confirmation bias in research leads to false conclusions and reproducibility crises.
