## Definition

Let:

- $W$ : A word or phrase
- $M_1, M_2$ : Different meanings of $W$
- $\text{Arg}$ : An argument using $W$

Then an argument commits the **Equivocation** fallacy if and only if:

$$
\text{Arg uses } W \text{ with meaning } M_1 \text{ in one place and } M_2 \text{ in another}
$$

> [!TIP] Interpretation
> The same word is used with different meanings within the same argument, creating a misleading conclusion.

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Theory Confusion
> **Scenario:** "Evolution is just a theory. In science, 'theory' means a well-substantiated explanation, but colloquially it means a guess, so evolution is just a guess."
>
> **Why it fails:** The argument switches between scientific and colloquial meanings of "theory" to reach a false conclusion. Scientific theories are supported by extensive evidence.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: The Man Argument
> **Scenario:** "No man is an island. Manhattan is an island. Therefore, Manhattan is not a man."
>
> **Why it fails:** The first "man" refers to human beings metaphorically; the conclusion uses "man" literally. The syllogism fails due to this semantic shift.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: The Right to Bear Arms
> **Scenario:** "I have a right to own this weapon because the law says so, and rights are by definition morally correct, so owning this weapon must be morally correct."
>
> **Why it fails:** "Right" shifts from legal entitlement to moral correctness. Legal rights and moral rights are different concepts that don't necessarily align.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: The Light Sleeper
> **Scenario:** "Nothing is better than a good night's sleep. A short nap is better than nothing. Therefore, a short nap is better than a good night's sleep."
>
> **Why it fails:** "Nothing" changes meaning between premises—from "no thing" in the first to "the absence of anything" in the second. This semantic slide invalidates the logic.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: The Bank Robbery
> **Scenario:** "I deposited money in the bank. The river bank is eroding. Therefore, my money is eroding."
>
> **Why it fails:** "Bank" refers to a financial institution in the first premise and a riverside in the second. These are homonyms with completely different meanings.
