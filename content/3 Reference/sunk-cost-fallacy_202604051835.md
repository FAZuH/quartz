## Definition

Let:
- $P$ : A course of action or investment
- $R$ : Resources already invested in $P$ (time, money, effort)

Then an argument commits the **Sunk Cost Fallacy** if and only if it asserts:
$$
\text{We've invested } R \text{ in } P \implies \text{We must continue with } P
$$

> [!NOTE]
> Sunk costs cannot be recovered and should not influence future decisions.

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Bad Movie
> **Scenario:** Someone has watched 90 minutes of a terrible 2-hour movie but stays until the end because "I've already invested this much time."
>
> **Why it fails:** The 90 minutes are gone regardless. Staying for 30 more minutes of suffering wastes additional time. The decision should be based on whether the remaining film is worth watching.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: The Failing Business
> **Scenario:** An entrepreneur has invested $100,000 in a business that's clearly failing. They invest another $50,000 because "I can't give up after putting in so much."
>
> **Why it fails:** The original $100,000 is lost regardless. The question is whether the additional $50,000 has better expected returns in this business or elsewhere.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: The Wrong Degree
> **Scenario:** A student is in their third year of a degree they hate and are failing. They refuse to switch majors because "I've already completed three years."
>
> **Why it fails:** Those three years are sunk. The decision should be based on whether completing this degree or switching leads to a better outcome for the remaining time and future career.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: The Concorde Fallacy (Original)
> **Scenario:** The British and French governments continued funding the Concorde project long after it was clear it wouldn't be profitable because they'd already spent so much.
>
> **Why it fails:** The development costs were sunk. The relevant question was whether future operation would be profitable, not how much had already been spent.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: The Toxic Relationship
> **Scenario:** Someone stays in an unhappy relationship because "We've been together for five years, I can't throw that away."
>
> **Why it fails:** Those five years are in the past regardless. The decision should be based on whether the relationship is likely to improve and make them happy going forward, not time already invested.
