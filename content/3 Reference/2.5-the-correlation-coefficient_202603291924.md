## Definition: Covariance

Let $(X, Y)$ have a joint distribution with means $\mu_1, \mu_2$ and variances $\sigma_1^2, \sigma_2^2$.

The **covariance** of $(X, Y)$ is defined as:

$$
\begin{align}
\text{Cov}(X, Y) & = E[(X - \mu_1)(Y - \mu_2)] \\
 & = E[XY] - \mu_{1}\mu_{2}
\end{align}
$$


## Definition: Correlation Coefficient

If $\sigma_1 > 0$ and $\sigma_2 > 0$, the **correlation coefficient** between $X$ and $Y$ is:

$$\rho = \frac{E[(X - \mu_1)(Y - \mu_2)]}{\sigma_1 \sigma_2} = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_1 \sigma_2}$$

Correlatino measures the <u>linear dependence</u> between 2 random variables.

## Properties

> [!INFO] Key Properties
> - $E(XY) = \mu_1 \mu_2 + \operatorname{cov}(X, Y) = \mu_1 \mu_2 + \rho \sigma_1 \sigma_2$
> - $-1 \leq \rho \leq 1$ (**Theorem 2.5.1**)
> - $\operatorname{cov}(X, Y) = 0$ when $X \perp Y$ (**Theorem 2.5.2**)
> - $\rho = 0$ does **not** imply independence (Example 2.5.3)

## Theorem 2.5.3: Linear Conditional Mean

If $E(Y|X)$ is linear in $X$, then:

$$E(Y|X) = \mu_2 + \rho \frac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1}(X - \mu_1)$$

And the conditional variance:

$$\operatorname{Var}(Y|X) = \sigma_2^2(1 - \rho^2)$$

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Discrete Case (Example 2.5.1)
> For coin flips: $X_1$ = heads on first two flips, $X_2$ = heads on all three flips.
> - $E(X_1) = 1$, $E(X_2) = 3/2$
> - $\sigma_1^2 = 1/2$, $\sigma_2^2 = 3/4$
> - $\operatorname{cov}(X_1, X_2) = 1/2$
> - $\rho = 0.816$

> [!EXAMPLE] Continuous Case (Example 2.5.2)
> $f(x,y) = x + y$, $0 < x < 1$, $0 < y < 1$:
> - $\mu_1 = \mu_2 = 7/12$
> - $\sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2 = 11/144$
> - $\operatorname{cov}(X,Y) = -1/144$
> - $\rho = -1/11$
