## Definition: Covariance

Let $(X, Y)$ have a joint distribution with means $\mu\_1, \mu\_2$ and variances $\sigma\_1^2, \sigma\_2^2$.

The **covariance** of $(X, Y)$ is defined as:

$$\operatorname{cov}(X, Y) = E\[(X - \mu\_1)(Y - \mu\_2)]$$

An equivalent formula (often easier to compute):

$$\operatorname{cov}(X, Y) = E(XY) - \mu\_1 \mu\_2$$

## Definition: Correlation Coefficient

If $\sigma\_1 > 0$ and $\sigma\_2 > 0$, the **correlation coefficient** between $X$ and $Y$ is:

$$\rho = \frac{E\[(X - \mu\_1)(Y - \mu\_2)]}{\sigma\_1 \sigma\_2} = \frac{\operatorname{cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma\_1 \sigma\_2}$$

## Properties

> [!INFO] Key Properties
>
> - $E(XY) = \mu\_1 \mu\_2 + \operatorname{cov}(X, Y) = \mu\_1 \mu\_2 + \rho \sigma\_1 \sigma\_2$
> - $-1 \leq \rho \leq 1$ (**Theorem 2.5.1**)
> - $\operatorname{cov}(X, Y) = 0$ when $X \perp Y$ (**Theorem 2.5.2**)
> - $\rho = 0$ does **not** imply independence (Example 2.5.3)

## Theorem 2.5.3: Linear Conditional Mean

If $E(Y|X)$ is linear in $X$, then:

$$E(Y|X) = \mu\_2 + \rho \frac{\sigma\_2}{\sigma\_1}(X - \mu\_1)$$

And the conditional variance:

$$\operatorname{Var}(Y|X) = \sigma\_2^2(1 - \rho^2)$$

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Discrete Case (Example 2.5.1)
> For coin flips: $X\_1$ = heads on first two flips, $X\_2$ = heads on all three flips.
>
> - $E(X\_1) = 1$, $E(X\_2) = 3/2$
> - $\sigma\_1^2 = 1/2$, $\sigma\_2^2 = 3/4$
> - $\operatorname{cov}(X\_1, X\_2) = 1/2$
> - $\rho = 0.816$

> [!EXAMPLE] Continuous Case (Example 2.5.2)
> $f(x,y) = x + y$, $0 < x < 1$, $0 < y < 1$:
>
> - $\mu\_1 = \mu\_2 = 7/12$
> - $\sigma\_1^2 = \sigma\_2^2 = 11/144$
> - $\operatorname{cov}(X,Y) = -1/144$
> - $\rho = -1/11$
