Vector Error Correction Model — a restricted VAR that incorporates cointegration relationships to combine short-run dynamics with long-run equilibrium.

## Definition

The **Vector Error Correction Model (VECM)** is a restricted form of [[Vector Autoregression (VAR)]] used when variables are non-stationary at level but are [[Cointegration|cointegrated]].

### When to Use VECM

Follow this decision tree for VAR specification:

1. Are all variables stationary at level? → **VAR in Level**
2. Are variables non-stationary at level but stationary after differencing?
   - No cointegration → **VAR in Difference**
   - Cointegration exists → **VECM** (Restricted VAR)

### Intuition

While cointegrated variables have a long-run equilibrium relationship, there may be short-run deviations. VECM **restricts** the VAR to ensure the system converges to the long-run cointegration relationship while still allowing short-run dynamic adjustments.

> [!TIP] Intuition
> The cointegration relationship acts like a "magnet" pulling the system back toward long-run equilibrium. The term "error correction" comes from the gradual correction of deviations through partial short-run adjustments.

### Connection to ECM

VECM is the **multivariate generalization** of the [[Error Correction Model (ECM)]]. When $n = 2$ variables with one cointegrating relationship, VECM reduces to the [[ECM Engle-Granger|Engle-Granger ECM]].

### Specification

A VECM includes:

- Lagged differences of all variables (short-run dynamics)
- **Error correction terms** representing the cointegrating relationships

$$
\Delta \boldsymbol{x}_t = \Pi \boldsymbol{x}_{t-1} + \sum_{i=1}^{p-1} \Gamma_i \Delta \boldsymbol{x}_{t-i} + \boldsymbol{e}_t
$$

where $\Pi = \alpha \beta'$ contains the cointegration vectors ($\beta$) and adjustment coefficients ($\alpha$).
