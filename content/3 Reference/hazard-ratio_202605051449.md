## Definition

The **Hazard Ratio (HR)** is the ratio of hazard functions between two subjects, measuring the relative risk associated with a covariate change.

## Categorical Predictor

Let $x_1$ be binary ($1$ = condition A, $0$ = otherwise), with other covariates held constant:

$$h_1(t) = h_0(t) e^{\beta_1 + \beta_2 x_2 + \cdots}, \quad h_2(t) = h_0(t) e^{0 + \beta_2 x_2 + \cdots}$$

The hazard ratio for condition A vs not-A:

$$HR = \frac{h_1(t)}{h_2(t)} = \frac{h_0(t) e^{\beta_1 + \beta_2 x_2 + \cdots}}{h_0(t) e^{\beta_2 x_2 + \cdots}} = e^{\beta_1}$$

## Numeric Predictor

Let $x_2$ be numeric. Compare subjects differing by 1 unit in $x_2$, all else equal:

$$HR = \frac{h_0(t) e^{\beta_1 x_1 + \beta_2(a+1) + \cdots}}{h_0(t) e^{\beta_1 x_1 + \beta_2(a) + \cdots}} = e^{\beta_2}$$

## Interpretation of $\beta$

| $\beta_j$ | $HR = e^{\beta_j}$ | Meaning |
|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| $\beta_j > 0$ | $HR > 1$ | Increasing $x_j$ by 1 unit **raises** hazard — higher risk |
| $\beta_j = 0$ | $HR = 1$ | $x_j$ has **no effect** on hazard |
| $\beta_j < 0$ | $HR < 1$ | Increasing $x_j$ by 1 unit **lowers** hazard — protective effect |

## Key Properties

1. **Constant over time**: $HR$ does not depend on $t$ (proportional hazards assumption)
2. **Multiplicative**: For a $\Delta$-unit change in $x_j$, $HR = e^{\beta_j \Delta}$
3. **Independent of $h_0(t)$**: HR depends only on $\beta$ and covariate differences

> [!TIP] Interpretation
> $HR = e^{\beta_j}$ means: for each 1-unit increase in $x_j$, the hazard is multiplied by $e^{\beta_j}$. An $HR$ of 2 means the event rate is twice as high; $HR = 0.5$ means it's halved.
