## Definition

Let:
- $A$ : An initial event
- $B, C, \dots, Z$ : A chain of subsequent events
- $Z$ : A catastrophic outcome

Then an argument commits the **Slippery Slope** fallacy if and only if it asserts:
$$
A \text{ happens} \implies B \implies C \implies \dots \implies Z \text{ (catastrophic)}
$$

> [!NOTE]
> This fallacy assumes an inevitable chain of events without sufficient evidence for each step.

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Censorship Slope
> **Scenario:** "If we ban hate speech, next we'll ban political criticism, then all speech will be controlled, and we'll live in a totalitarian state."
>
> **Why it fails:** Each step requires separate justification. Banning one category of speech doesn't automatically lead to banning all speech. Intermediate steps can be halted.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: The Drug Legalization Argument
> **Scenario:** "If we legalize marijuana, people will start using harder drugs, become addicted, commit crimes, and society will collapse."
>
> **Why it fails:** This assumes marijuana use inevitably leads to hard drug use without evidence. Many people use marijuana without progressing to other substances.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: The Technology Ban
> **Scenario:** "If we allow AI to write essays, students will stop learning entirely, lose critical thinking skills, and become intellectually dependent on machines."
>
> **Why it fails:** The chain assumes worst-case outcomes at each step without evidence. AI tools can be integrated thoughtfully into education without these extremes.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: The Tax Increase Fear
> **Scenario:** "If we raise taxes on the wealthy by 2%, next year it'll be 5%, then 10%, then 50%, until nobody has any incentive to work."
>
> **Why it fails:** Each tax change requires separate political processes and public support. Small increases don't automatically lead to confiscatory rates.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: The Privacy Erosion
> **Scenario:** "If we install security cameras in public parks, soon they'll be in streets, then homes, then we'll have total surveillance with no privacy."
>
> **Why it fails:** While privacy concerns are valid, each expansion of surveillance requires separate justification and faces opposition. The slope isn't necessarily slippery.
