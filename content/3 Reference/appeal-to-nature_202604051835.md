## Definition

Let:
- $P$ : A proposition or action
- $\text{Natural}(P)$ : $P$ is natural

Then an argument commits the **Appeal to Nature** fallacy if and only if it asserts:
$$
\text{Natural}(P) \implies P \text{ is good/correct}
$$

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: Natural Foods Marketing
> **Scenario:** A product is advertised as "100% natural" and therefore healthier than alternatives, implying natural substances are always better for you.
>
> **Why it fails:** Many natural substances are toxic (arsenic, botulinum toxin, poisonous mushrooms) while many synthetic substances are beneficial (vaccines, antibiotics, purified water treatment).

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: Appeal to Primitive Diet
> **Scenario:** Someone argues we should eat only what our ancestors ate 10,000 years ago because "that's what humans evolved to eat."
>
> **Why it fails:** Human life expectancy was much shorter 10,000 years ago, and modern nutrition science has improved our understanding of balanced diets. Natural doesn't mean optimal.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: Alternative Medicine Claims
> **Scenario:** A treatment is promoted as superior because it uses "natural herbs" instead of "chemicals," appealing to the idea that natural equals safe.
>
> **Why it fails:** Everything is made of chemicals, including natural herbs. Many natural plants are deadly poisonous, and many synthetic medicines save lives. Efficacy matters, not origin.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: Anti-Technology Arguments
> **Scenario:** Someone argues that children shouldn't use computers for learning because "playing outside is more natural."
>
> **Why it fails:** While outdoor play has benefits, "naturalness" isn't a valid criterion for educational value. Books aren't natural either, but they're valuable learning tools.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: Natural Selection Ethics
> **Scenario:** An argument that social welfare programs are wrong because they interfere with "natural selection" and survival of the fittest.
>
> **Why it fails:** Just because something occurs in nature (disease, predation, death) doesn't make it morally good. Humans regularly intervene in natural processes for ethical reasons.
