## Remark: Facts about echelon forms

1. Every matrix has unique RREF
2. REF are not unique
3. RREF, and all REF have the same number of zero rows, and the leading 1's always occur in the same positions. These positions are called the **pivot positions**. A column that contains a pivot position is called a **pivot column**. ^def-pivot-position-column

For example, consider the following augmented matrix:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 & -5 & 3 & 6 & 14 \\
0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & -\frac{7}{2} & -6 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The leading 1's always occur in positions (row, column): (1,1), (2,3), and (3, 5). These are the pivot positions. The pivot columns are columns 1, 3, and 5.

## Theorem: Solution of homogeneous linear system

> Every homogeneous linear system has a trivial solution, and thus [[1.1 Introduction to Systems of Linear Equations#Definition Consistency of linear system|consistent]]. As a corollary of that, there are only two possibilities for its solutions:
>
> 1. The system has only the trivial solution
> 2. The system has infinitely many solutions, in addition to the trivial solution

![[assets/Pasted image 20250713060205.png]]

## Theorem 1.2.1: Free variable for homogeneous system

> Let $\mathbf{Ax}=\mathbf{b}$ : Homogeneous linear system with $n$ [[1.1 Introduction to Systems of Linear Equations#^def-unknowns|unknowns]]
>
> If the reduced row echelon form of $\mathbf{Ax}=\mathbf{b}$ has $r$ nonzero rows
>
> Then $\mathbf{Ax}=\mathbf{b}$ has $n-r$ free variables

Recall the properties of RREF. As a result,

- Each of $r$ nonzero row hold 1 leading 1
- Each leading 1 take up 1 column

Thus, any remaining "free" columns, which is $n$ columns taken up by $r$ leading 1's are free variables.

## Theorem 1.2.2

> A homogeneous linear system with more unknowns than equations has infinitely many solutions.

Consider a matrix with $m$ equations (row) in $n$ unknowns (column), and $m<n$. Because there are more columns than nonzero rows, there are simply not enough nonzero rows to "take up" the columns. Therefore, there are always at least $n-m$ free variables remaining.

More formally,

> [!proof]
> Consider $\mathbf{Ax} = \mathbf{0}$ with $m$ equations and $n$ unknowns, where $n>m$.
>
> In RREF, the number of nonzero rows $r\leq m$ (can't exceed the number of equations).
>
> By Theorem 1.2.1, there are $n-r$ free variables. Since $n>m\geq r$, we have $n-r\geq n-m>0$
>
> Therefore, there is at least one free variable, giving infinitely many solutions
