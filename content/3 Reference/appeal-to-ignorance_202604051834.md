## Definition

Let:
- $P$ : A proposition

Then an argument commits the **Appeal to Ignorance** if and only if it asserts:
$$
\text{There is no proof that } P \text{ is true} \implies P \text{ is false}
$$

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Courtroom Defense
> **Scenario:** A lawyer argues that their client must be innocent because the prosecution cannot prove they committed the crime beyond all possible doubt.
>
> **Why it fails:** Lack of proof of guilt doesn't establish innocence. The absence of evidence for one position doesn't automatically make the opposite position true.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: Paranormal Claims
> **Scenario:** Someone claims that ghosts must exist because science hasn't proven they don't exist.
>
> **Why it fails:** The burden of proof lies with the person making the positive claim. Not having disproven something isn't the same as having proven it.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: Historical Revisionism
> **Scenario:** A historian argues that a particular ancient civilization didn't have advanced mathematics because no records of it have been found.
>
> **Why it fails:** Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. Records could have been lost, destroyed, or not yet discovered. More research is needed before drawing conclusions.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: Medical Testing
> **Scenario:** A patient insists a treatment works because "doctors can't prove it doesn't work."
>
> **Why it fails:** Medical treatments need positive evidence of efficacy, not just lack of proof that they're ineffective. The burden is to show it works, not that it might not not-work.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: Conspiracy Theories
> **Scenario:** Someone argues that a conspiracy must be real because the government hasn't released documents proving it didn't happen.
>
> **Why it fails:** Governments don't typically prove negatives, and lack of disproof doesn't constitute proof. Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence, not just gaps in documentation.
