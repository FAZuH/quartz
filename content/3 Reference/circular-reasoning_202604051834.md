## Definition

Let:
- $P$ : A proposition

Then an argument commits the **Circular Reasoning** fallacy if and only if it asserts:
$$
P \text{ is true because } P \text{ is true}
$$

> [!TIP] Interpretation
> The conclusion is used as evidence for itself, providing no independent support.

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Dictionary Definition
> **Scenario:** Someone defines a term using another term, and when asked to define the second term, they use the first term again.
>
> **Why it fails:** "X means Y, and Y means X" provides no actual information about what X or Y mean. It's a definitional circle with no grounding.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: The Prophecy is True
> **Scenario:** "The prophecy must be true because the sacred text says so, and the sacred text is reliable because it was written by the prophet who made the prophecy."
>
> **Why it fails:** The reliability of the text depends on the prophecy being true, but the prophecy's truth depends on the text being reliable. No independent verification exists.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: The Conspiracy Theory
> **Scenario:** "The media can't be trusted because they're part of the conspiracy, and we know there's a conspiracy because the media never report on it."
>
> **Why it fails:** The conclusion (media untrustworthiness due to conspiracy) is used as evidence for the premise (the conspiracy exists). This creates an unfalsifiable loop.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: Logical Validity
> **Scenario:** "My argument is valid because the conclusion follows from the premises, and the premises are true because they support my valid conclusion."
>
> **Why it fails:** Validity and truth are being used to justify each other. An argument needs independent evidence for its premises and proper logical structure, not mutual reinforcement.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: The Orator's Skill
> **Scenario:** "He must be a great orator because he always wins debates, and he wins debates because he's a great orator."
>
> **Why it fails:** This offers no explanation of what makes him skilled—perhaps he wins through emotional manipulation, misleading statistics, or opponents' weaknesses rather than oratorical excellence.
