## Proof: Confidence interval for difference in mean, known inequal variances

Let

- $X,Y$ : [[Def-random-variable|Random variable]]
- $\mu_{1}$ : [[Def-mean|Mean]] of $X$
- $\mu_{2}$ : Mean of $Y$
- $\Delta= \mu_{1}-\mu_{2}$
- $\sigma_{1}^2 = \operatorname{Var}(X),\; \sigma_{2}^2=\operatorname{Var}(Y)$ known

Let

- $X_{1},\dots,X_{n_{1}}$ : [[Def-random-sample|Random sample]] from $X$
- $Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n_{2}}$ : Random sample from $Y$
- $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n_1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}X_{i}$ : [[common-distribution-equations_202507221712#Sample Mean and Variance Distributions|Sample mean]] of $X_{i}$
- $\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{n_2}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{2}}Y_{i}$ : Sample mean of $Y_{i}$

Assume the random samples $X_{i}$ and $Y_{i}$ are independent.

Assume $X\sim N(\mu_{1},\sigma_{1}^2)$ and $Y\sim N(\mu_{2},\sigma_{2}^2)$.

Then $\bar{X}\sim N\left( \mu_{1}, \dfrac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} \right)$ and $\bar{Y}\sim N\left( \mu_{2}, \dfrac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} \right)$

Let

- $\hat{\Delta}=\bar{X}-\bar{Y}$

Then $E(\hat{\Delta})=E(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})=E(\bar{X})-E(\bar{Y})=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}$, thus $\hat{\Delta}$ is an [[Def-unbiased-estimator|unbiased estimator]] of $\Delta$.

Additionally, $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\Delta})  = \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})  = \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}) + \operatorname{Var}(\bar{Y})  = \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}$

By independence of the random samples, we may obtain the distribution for $\hat{\Delta}$ by standardization:

$$
\begin{align}
\bar{X}-\bar{Y} = \hat{\Delta} & \sim N\left( \mu_{1}-\mu_{2}, \frac{\sigma^2_{1}}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma^2_{2}}{n_{2}} \right) \\
 \iff  \frac{\hat{\Delta}- (\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \\
 \iff  \frac{\hat{\Delta}- \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \\
\end{align}
$$

Thus the $(1-\alpha)100\%$ [[def-confidence-interval_202507220823|confidence interval]] for $\Delta$ is

$$
\begin{align}
1-\alpha & = P\left( -z_{\alpha/2} < \frac{\hat{\Delta}- \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }} < z_{\alpha/2} \right) \\
 
 & = P\left( -z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}} < \hat{\Delta}- \Delta < z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }\right) \\
 
 & = P\left( -\hat{\Delta} - z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}} < - \Delta < - \hat{\Delta} + z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }\right) \\
 
 & = P\left( \hat{\Delta} - z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}} < \Delta < \hat{\Delta} + z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }\right) \\
 
 & = P\left( \hat{\Delta}-z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}} < \mu_{1}-\mu_{2} < \hat{\Delta}+z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }\right) \\
\end{align}
$$

From the last result, we can see that the following interval is an exact $(1-\alpha)100\%$ confidence interval for $\Delta=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}$

$$
\left( 
	(\bar{x}-\bar{y}) - z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} },\;\;
	(\bar{x}-\bar{y}) + z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }
\right)
$$

## Proof: Confidence interval for difference in mean, unknown, inequal variances

Let

- $X,Y$ : [[Def-random-variable|Random variable]]
- $\mu_{1}$ : [[Def-mean|Mean]] of $X$
- $\mu_{2}$ : Mean of $Y$
- $\Delta= \mu_{1}-\mu_{2}$
- $\sigma_{1}^2 = \operatorname{Var}(X),\; \sigma_{2}^2=\operatorname{Var}(Y)$ exists

Let

- $X_{1},\dots,X_{n_{1}}$ : [[Def-random-sample|Random sample]] from $X$
- $Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n_{2}}$ : Random sample from $Y$
- $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}X_{i}$ : [[common-distribution-equations_202507221712#Sample Mean and Variance Distributions|Sample mean]] of $X_{i}$
- $\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}Y_{i}$ : Sample mean of $Y_{i}$

Assume the random samples $X_{i}$ and $Y_{i}$ are independent.

Then $\bar{X}\sim N\left( \mu_{1}, \dfrac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} \right)$ and $\bar{Y}\sim N\left( \mu_{2}, \dfrac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} \right)$

Let

- $\hat{\Delta}=\bar{X}-\bar{Y}$

Then $E(\hat{\Delta})=E(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})=E(\bar{X})-E(\bar{Y})=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}$, thus $\hat{\Delta}$ is an [[Def-unbiased-estimator|unbiased estimator]] of $\Delta$.
Additionally, $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\Delta})  = \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})  = \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}) + \operatorname{Var}(\bar{Y})  = \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}$

Let

- $S_{1}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{n_{1}-1}\sum^{n_{1}}_{i=1}(X_{i}-\bar{X})^2$ : [[common-distribution-equations_202507221712#Sample Mean and Variance Distributions|Sample variance]] of $X_{i}$
- $S_{2}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{n_{2}-1}\sum^{n_{2}}_{i=1}(Y_{i}-\bar{Y})^2$ : Sample variance of $Y_{i}$

Then $S_{1}^2\sim N\left(\sigma^2, \dfrac{2\sigma^4}{n-1}\right)$ and $S_{2}^2\sim N\left(\sigma_{2}^2, \dfrac{2\sigma_{2}^4}{n_{2}-1}\right)$

By independence of the random samples, we may obtain the distribution for $\hat{\Delta}$ by standartization:

$$
\begin{align}
\bar{X}-\bar{Y} = \hat{\Delta} & \sim N\left( \mu_{1}-\mu_{2}, \frac{\sigma^2_{1}}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma^2_{2}}{n_{2}} \right) \\
 \iff  \frac{\hat{\Delta}- (\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \\
 \iff  \frac{\hat{\Delta}- \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \\
\end{align}
$$

Thus similar to [[4.2-confidence-intervals_202507220822#Example 4.2.1 Confidence interval for $ mu$ under normality|Example 4.2.1]], its $(1-\alpha)100\%$ confidence interfal for $\Delta$ is

$$
\begin{align}
1-\alpha & = P\left( z_{\alpha/2} < \frac{\hat{\Delta}- \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }} < -z_{\alpha/2} \right) \\
 
 & = P\left( z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}} < \hat{\Delta}- \Delta < - z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }\right) \\
 
 & = P\left( -\hat{\Delta} + z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}} < - \Delta < - \hat{\Delta} - z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }\right) \\
 
 & = P\left( \hat{\Delta} - z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}} < \Delta < \hat{\Delta} + z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }\right) \\
 
 & = P\left( \hat{\Delta}-z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}}} < \mu_{1}-\mu_{2} < \hat{\Delta}+z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }\right) \\
\end{align}
$$

By estimating the variance by the sample variances, this leads to the approximate $(1-\alpha)100\%$ confidence interval for $\Delta=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}$ given by
$\left( (\bar{x}-\bar{y}) - z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{ \frac{s_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{s_{1}^2}{n_{1}} }, (\bar{x}-\bar{y}) + z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{ \frac{s_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{s_{1}^2}{n_{1}} } \right)$

## Proof: Confidence interval for difference in mean, unknown, equal variances

Continuing from the [[#Proof Confidence interval for difference in mean, unknown, inequal variances|previous example]], assume $\sigma_{1}^2=\sigma^2_{2}=\sigma^2$. Thus the distributions can differ only in location, i.e., a **location model**

Assume $X\sim N(\mu_{1},\sigma^2)$ and $Y\sim N(\mu_{2},\sigma^2)$.

> [!tip]
> May be through [[theorem-central-limit-theorem_202509190924|Central Limit Theorem]]

Continuing from the previous example

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\hat{\Delta} - \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma_{1}^2}{n_{1}} + \frac{\sigma_{2}^2}{n_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \nonumber \\
\frac{(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})}{\sqrt{ \sigma^2\left( \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} \right) }} & \sim N(0,1) \nonumber \\
\frac{(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})}{\sigma\sqrt{ \left( \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} \right) }} & \sim N(0,1) \tag{1}
\end{align}
$$

Let
$S_{p}^2= \frac{(n_{1}-1)S_{1}^2+(n_{2}-1)S_{2}^2}{n_{1}+n_{2}-2}$

Recall $E(S_{1}^{2})=\sigma_{1}^2,\;E(S_{2}^{2})=\sigma_{2}^2,\;\sigma_{1}^2=\sigma_{2}^2=\sigma^2$. As a result,

$$
\begin{align}
E(S^2_{p}) & = E\left[\frac{(n_{1}-1)S_{1}^2+(n_{2}-1)S_{2}^2}{n_{1}+n_{2}-2}\right] \\
 
 & = \frac{E\left[(n_{1}-1)S_{1}^2+(n_{2}-1)S_{2}^2\right]}{n_{1}+n_{2}-2}  \\
  
 & = \frac{E\left[(n_{1}-1)S_{1}^2\right] + E\left[(n_{2}-1)S_{2}^2\right]}{n_{1}+n_{2}-2}  \\
 
 & = \frac{(n_{1}-1)E(S_{1}^2) + (n_{2}-1)E(S_{2}^2)}{n_{1}+n_{2}-2} \\

 & = \frac{(n_{1}-1)\sigma^2_{1} + (n_{2}-1)\sigma^2_{2}}{n_{1}+n_{2}-2} \\
 
 & = \frac{\sigma^2[(n_{1}-1) + (n_{2}-1)]}{n_{1}+n_{2}-2} \\
 
 & = \frac{\sigma^2(n_{1}+n_{2}-2)}{n_{1}+n_{2}-2} \\
 
 & = \sigma^2 \\
\end{align}
$$

Therefore, $S_{p}^2$ is an [[Def-unbiased-estimator|unbiased estimator]] of $\sigma^2$. We call it the **pooled estimator** of $\sigma^2$.

By [[common-distribution-equations_202507221712#Normal Distribution Relationships|Normal Distribution Relationships]], $(n_{1}-1)S^2_{1}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2(n_{1}-1)$ and $(n_{2}-1)S^2_{2}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2(n_{2}-1)$. Also, $S_{1}^2$ and $S_{2}^2$ are independent. Therefore, by [[3.3.1-the-chi-square-distribution_202507240802#Corollary 3.3.1|Corollary 3.3.1]],

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{(n_{1}-1)S_{1}^2}{\sigma^2} + \frac{(n_{2}-1)S_{2}^2}{\sigma^2} & \sim \chi^2(n_{1}-1 + n_{2}-2) \\
\frac{S_{p}^2(n-2)}{\sigma^2} & \sim \chi^2(n-2)
\end{align}
$$

Finally, because $S_{1}^2$ and $S_{2}^2$ is independent of $\bar{X}$ and $\bar{Y}$ respectively, and the random samples are independent of each other, it follows that $S_{p}^2$ is independent of expression $(1)$.

Thus by [[3.6.1-the-t-distribution_202507240804|3.6.1 The t-distribution]] we may construct a random variable with t-distribution:

$$
\begin{align}
T & = \frac{[(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})]/\sigma \sqrt{ \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} }}{\sqrt{ (n-2)S_{p}^2/(n-2)\sigma^2 }} \\
 & = \frac{(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})}{S_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} }}\quad \sim t_{n-2}
\end{align}
$$

The confidence interval may then be found:

$$
\begin{align}
1-\alpha & = P\left( t_{n-2}< \frac{(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})}{S_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} }} < t_{n_{2}} \right) \\
 
 & = P\left( t_{n-2}S_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} }< (\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}) < t_{n_{2}}S_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} } \right) \\

 & = P\left( (\bar{x}-\bar{y})- t_{n-2}S_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} }< \mu_{1}-\mu_{2} < (\bar{x}-\bar{y} )+ t_{n_{2}}S_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} } \right) \\
\end{align}
$$

From the last result, we can see that the following interval is an exact $(1-\alpha)100\%$ [[def-confidence-interval_202507220823|confidence interval]] for $\Delta=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}$

$$
\left( 
	(\bar{x}-\bar{y})-t_{(\alpha/2,n-2)}s_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} },\;\;
	(\bar{x}-\bar{y})+t_{(\alpha/2,n-2)}s_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}} },\;\;
\right)
$$
