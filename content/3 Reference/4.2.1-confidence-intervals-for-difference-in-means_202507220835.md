## Proof: Confidence interval for difference in mean, known inequal variances

Let

- $X,Y$ : [[Def-random-variable|Random variable]]
- $\mu\_{1}$ : [[Def-mean|Mean]] of $X$
- $\mu\_{2}$ : Mean of $Y$
- $\Delta= \mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}$
- $\sigma\_{1}^2 = \operatorname{Var}(X),; \sigma\_{2}^2=\operatorname{Var}(Y)$ known

Let

- $X\_{1},\dots,X\_{n\_{1}}$ : [[Def-random-sample|Random sample]] from $X$
- $Y\_{1},\dots,Y\_{n\_{2}}$ : Random sample from $Y$
- $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n\_1}\sum\_{i=1}^{n\_{1}}X\_{i}$ : [[common-distribution-equations_202507221712#Sample Mean and Variance Distributions|Sample mean]] of $X\_{i}$
- $\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{n\_2}\sum\_{i=1}^{n\_{2}}Y\_{i}$ : Sample mean of $Y\_{i}$

Assume the random samples $X\_{i}$ and $Y\_{i}$ are independent.

Assume $X\sim N(\mu\_{1},\sigma\_{1}^2)$ and $Y\sim N(\mu\_{2},\sigma\_{2}^2)$.

Then $\bar{X}\sim N\left( \mu\_{1}, \dfrac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} \right)$ and $\bar{Y}\sim N\left( \mu\_{2}, \dfrac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} \right)$

Let

- $\hat{\Delta}=\bar{X}-\bar{Y}$

Then $E(\hat{\Delta})=E(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})=E(\bar{X})-E(\bar{Y})=\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}$, thus $\hat{\Delta}$ is an [[Def-unbiased-estimator|unbiased estimator]] of $\Delta$.

Additionally, $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\Delta})  = \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})  = \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}) + \operatorname{Var}(\bar{Y})  = \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}$

By independence of the random samples, we may obtain the distribution for $\hat{\Delta}$ by standardization:
$$
\begin{align}
\bar{X}-\bar{Y} = \hat{\Delta} & \sim N\left( \mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}, \frac{\sigma^2\_{1}}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma^2\_{2}}{n\_{2}} \right) \\
\iff  \frac{\hat{\Delta}- (\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2})}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \\
\iff  \frac{\hat{\Delta}- \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \\
\end{align}
$$

Thus the $(1-\alpha)100%$ [[def-confidence-interval_202507220823|confidence interval]] for $\Delta$ is
$$
\begin{align}
1-\alpha & = P\left( -z\_{\alpha/2} < \frac{\hat{\Delta}- \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }} < z\_{\alpha/2} \right) \\

& = P\left( -z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}} < \hat{\Delta}- \Delta < z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}}+\frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }\right) \\

& = P\left( -\hat{\Delta} - z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}} < - \Delta < - \hat{\Delta} + z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}}+\frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }\right) \\

& = P\left( \hat{\Delta} - z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}} < \Delta < \hat{\Delta} + z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}}+\frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }\right) \\

& = P\left( \hat{\Delta}-z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}} < \mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2} < \hat{\Delta}+z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}}+\frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }\right) \\
\end{align}
$$

From the last result, we can see that the following interval is an exact $(1-\alpha)100%$ confidence interval for $\Delta=\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}$
$$
\left(
(\bar{x}-\bar{y}) - z\_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} },;;
(\bar{x}-\bar{y}) + z\_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }
\right)
$$

## Proof: Confidence interval for difference in mean, unknown, inequal variances

Let

- $X,Y$ : [[Def-random-variable|Random variable]]
- $\mu\_{1}$ : [[Def-mean|Mean]] of $X$
- $\mu\_{2}$ : Mean of $Y$
- $\Delta= \mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}$
- $\sigma\_{1}^2 = \operatorname{Var}(X),; \sigma\_{2}^2=\operatorname{Var}(Y)$ exists

Let

- $X\_{1},\dots,X\_{n\_{1}}$ : [[Def-random-sample|Random sample]] from $X$
- $Y\_{1},\dots,Y\_{n\_{2}}$ : Random sample from $Y$
- $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum\_{i=1}^{n\_{1}}X\_{i}$ : [[common-distribution-equations_202507221712#Sample Mean and Variance Distributions|Sample mean]] of $X\_{i}$
- $\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{n}\sum\_{i=1}^{n\_{1}}Y\_{i}$ : Sample mean of $Y\_{i}$

Assume the random samples $X\_{i}$ and $Y\_{i}$ are independent.

Then $\bar{X}\sim N\left( \mu\_{1}, \dfrac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} \right)$ and $\bar{Y}\sim N\left( \mu\_{2}, \dfrac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} \right)$

Let

- $\hat{\Delta}=\bar{X}-\bar{Y}$

Then $E(\hat{\Delta})=E(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})=E(\bar{X})-E(\bar{Y})=\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}$, thus $\hat{\Delta}$ is an [[Def-unbiased-estimator|unbiased estimator]] of $\Delta$.
Additionally, $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\Delta})  = \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})  = \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}) + \operatorname{Var}(\bar{Y})  = \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}$

Let

- $S\_{1}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{n\_{1}-1}\sum^{n\_{1}}_{i=1}(X_{i}-\bar{X})^2$ : [[common-distribution-equations_202507221712#Sample Mean and Variance Distributions|Sample variance]] of $X\_{i}$
- $S\_{2}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{n\_{2}-1}\sum^{n\_{2}}_{i=1}(Y_{i}-\bar{Y})^2$ : Sample variance of $Y\_{i}$

Then $S\_{1}^2\sim N\left(\sigma^2, \dfrac{2\sigma^4}{n-1}\right)$ and $S\_{2}^2\sim N\left(\sigma\_{2}^2, \dfrac{2\sigma\_{2}^4}{n\_{2}-1}\right)$

By independence of the random samples, we may obtain the distribution for $\hat{\Delta}$ by standartization:
$$
\begin{align}
\bar{X}-\bar{Y} = \hat{\Delta} & \sim N\left( \mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}, \frac{\sigma^2\_{1}}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma^2\_{2}}{n\_{2}} \right) \\
\iff  \frac{\hat{\Delta}- (\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2})}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \\
\iff  \frac{\hat{\Delta}- \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \\
\end{align}
$$

Thus similar to [[4.2-confidence-intervals_202507220822#Example 4.2.1 Confidence interval for $ mu$ under normality|Example 4.2.1]], its $(1-\alpha)100%$ confidence interfal for $\Delta$ is
$$
\begin{align}
1-\alpha & = P\left( z\_{\alpha/2} < \frac{\hat{\Delta}- \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }} < -z\_{\alpha/2} \right) \\

& = P\left( z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}} < \hat{\Delta}- \Delta < - z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}}+\frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }\right) \\

& = P\left( -\hat{\Delta} + z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}} < - \Delta < - \hat{\Delta} - z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}}+\frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }\right) \\

& = P\left( \hat{\Delta} - z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}} < \Delta < \hat{\Delta} + z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}}+\frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }\right) \\

& = P\left( \hat{\Delta}-z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}}} < \mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2} < \hat{\Delta}+z\_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}}+\frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }\right) \\
\end{align}
$$

By estimating the variance by the sample variances, this leads to the approximate $(1-\alpha)100%$ confidence interval for $\Delta=\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}$ given by
$$ \left( (\bar{x}-\bar{y}) - z\_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{ \frac{s\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{s\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} }, (\bar{x}-\bar{y}) + z\_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{ \frac{s\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{s\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} } \right) $$

## Proof: Confidence interval for difference in mean, unknown, equal variances

Continuing from the [[#Proof Confidence interval for difference in mean, unknown, inequal variances|previous example]], assume $\sigma\_{1}^2=\sigma^2\_{2}=\sigma^2$. Thus the distributions can differ only in location, i.e., a **location model**

Assume $X\sim N(\mu\_{1},\sigma^2)$ and $Y\sim N(\mu\_{2},\sigma^2)$.

> [!tip]
> May be through [[theorem-central-limit-theorem_202509190924|Central Limit Theorem]]

Continuing from the previous example
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\hat{\Delta} - \Delta}{\sqrt{ \frac{\sigma\_{1}^2}{n\_{1}} + \frac{\sigma\_{2}^2}{n\_{2}} }} & \sim N(0, 1) \nonumber \\
\frac{(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2})}{\sqrt{ \sigma^2\left( \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} \right) }} & \sim N(0,1) \nonumber \\
\frac{(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2})}{\sigma\sqrt{ \left( \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} \right) }} & \sim N(0,1) \tag{1}
\end{align}
$$

Let
$$ S\_{p}^2= \frac{(n\_{1}-1)S\_{1}^2+(n\_{2}-1)S\_{2}^2}{n\_{1}+n\_{2}-2} $$

Recall $E(S\_{1}^{2})=\sigma\_{1}^2,;E(S\_{2}^{2})=\sigma\_{2}^2,;\sigma\_{1}^2=\sigma\_{2}^2=\sigma^2$. As a result,
$$
\begin{align}
E(S^2\_{p}) & = E\left\[\frac{(n\_{1}-1)S\_{1}^2+(n\_{2}-1)S\_{2}^2}{n\_{1}+n\_{2}-2}\right] \\

& = \frac{E\left\[(n\_{1}-1)S\_{1}^2+(n\_{2}-1)S\_{2}^2\right]}{n\_{1}+n\_{2}-2}  \\

& = \frac{E\left\[(n\_{1}-1)S\_{1}^2\right] + E\left\[(n\_{2}-1)S\_{2}^2\right]}{n\_{1}+n\_{2}-2}  \\

& = \frac{(n\_{1}-1)E(S\_{1}^2) + (n\_{2}-1)E(S\_{2}^2)}{n\_{1}+n\_{2}-2} \\

& = \frac{(n\_{1}-1)\sigma^2\_{1} + (n\_{2}-1)\sigma^2\_{2}}{n\_{1}+n\_{2}-2} \\

& = \frac{\sigma^2\[(n\_{1}-1) + (n\_{2}-1)]}{n\_{1}+n\_{2}-2} \\

& = \frac{\sigma^2(n\_{1}+n\_{2}-2)}{n\_{1}+n\_{2}-2} \\

& = \sigma^2 \\
\end{align}
$$

Therefore, $S\_{p}^2$ is an [[Def-unbiased-estimator|unbiased estimator]] of $\sigma^2$. We call it the **pooled estimator** of $\sigma^2$.

By [[common-distribution-equations_202507221712#Normal Distribution Relationships|Normal Distribution Relationships]], $(n\_{1}-1)S^2\_{1}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2(n\_{1}-1)$ and $(n\_{2}-1)S^2\_{2}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2(n\_{2}-1)$. Also, $S\_{1}^2$ and $S\_{2}^2$ are independent. Therefore, by [[3.3.1-the-chi-square-distribution_202507240802#Corollary 3.3.1|Corollary 3.3.1]],
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{(n\_{1}-1)S\_{1}^2}{\sigma^2} + \frac{(n\_{2}-1)S\_{2}^2}{\sigma^2} & \sim \chi^2(n\_{1}-1 + n\_{2}-2) \\
\frac{S\_{p}^2(n-2)}{\sigma^2} & \sim \chi^2(n-2)
\end{align}
$$

Finally, because $S\_{1}^2$ and $S\_{2}^2$ is independent of $\bar{X}$ and $\bar{Y}$ respectively, and the random samples are independent of each other, it follows that $S\_{p}^2$ is independent of expression $(1)$.

Thus by [[3.6.1-the-t-distribution_202507240804|3.6.1 The t-distribution]] we may construct a random variable with t-distribution:
$$
\begin{align}
T & = \frac{\[(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2})]/\sigma \sqrt{ \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} }}{\sqrt{ (n-2)S\_{p}^2/(n-2)\sigma^2 }} \\
& = \frac{(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2})}{S\_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} }}\quad \sim t\_{n-2}
\end{align}
$$

The confidence interval may then be found:
$$
\begin{align}
1-\alpha & = P\left( t\_{n-2}< \frac{(\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2})}{S\_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} }} < t\_{n\_{2}} \right) \\

& = P\left( t\_{n-2}S\_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} }< (\bar{X}-\bar{Y})-(\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}) < t\_{n\_{2}}S\_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} } \right) \\

& = P\left( (\bar{x}-\bar{y})- t\_{n-2}S\_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} }< \mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2} < (\bar{x}-\bar{y} )+ t\_{n\_{2}}S\_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} } \right) \\
\end{align}
$$

From the last result, we can see that the following interval is an exact $(1-\alpha)100%$ [[def-confidence-interval_202507220823|confidence interval]] for $\Delta=\mu\_{1}-\mu\_{2}$
$$
\left(
(\bar{x}-\bar{y})-t\_{(\alpha/2,n-2)}s\_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} },;;
(\bar{x}-\bar{y})+t\_{(\alpha/2,n-2)}s\_{p}\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n\_{1}}+\frac{1}{n\_{2}} },;;
\right)
$$
