## Definition

Let:
- $A, B$ : Two presented options
- $C$ : Other possible alternatives that exist

Then an argument commits the **False Dichotomy** (False Dilemma) fallacy if and only if it asserts:
$$
\text{Only } A \text{ or } B \text{ exist} \implies \text{Must choose } A \text{ or } B
$$

> [!NOTE]
> This fallacy ignores that more alternatives ($C$) may be possible.

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Political Binary
> **Scenario:** "You're either with us or against us. There's no middle ground on this issue."
>
> **Why it fails:** Most political issues have nuanced positions, compromises, partial agreements, and alternative approaches. Forcing a binary choice is reductive and manipulative.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: The Career Choice
> **Scenario:** "You have to choose between being a starving artist or a soulless corporate drone."
>
> **Why it fails:** These aren't the only options. Many artists earn comfortable livings, and many corporate workers find fulfillment. Creative careers and business careers exist on spectrums.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: The Security vs Privacy Trade-off
> **Scenario:** "We must sacrifice privacy for security. Which do you value more?"
>
> **Why it fails:** This presents a false trade-off. Effective security measures often don't require major privacy sacrifices, and many solutions balance both values.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: The Relationship Ultimatum
> **Scenario:** "Either commit to marriage right now or we're breaking up forever."
>
> **Why it fails:** Other options exist: continuing to date, moving in together, taking time apart, or just having an honest conversation about readiness. Ultimatums obscure alternatives.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: The Economic Systems
> **Scenario:** "Capitalism has failed, so we must adopt socialism instead."
>
> **Why it fails:** Economic systems exist on continua with many hybrid models. Mixed economies, market socialism, and regulated capitalism are alternatives that aren't pure versions of either extreme.
