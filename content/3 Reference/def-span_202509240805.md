## Definition

Let

- $V$ : [[def-vector-space-axioms_202509240715|Vector space]]
- $S=\{ \mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{r} \}$ : [[def-set_202510022014|Set]] of vectors in $V$
- $W$ : [[def-subspace_202509240725|Subspace]] of $V$

If $W$ consists of all possible [[def-linear-combination_202510020342|linear combinations]] of the vectors in $S$

Then

- We say $W$ is the subspace of $V$ **generated** or **spanned** by $S$
- We say the vectors $\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{r}$ **span** $W$
- We say the set $S$ **span** $W$
- We denote $W=\operatorname{span}\{ \mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{r} \} = \text{span(S)}$

## Interpretation

Look at the definition

By definition, if $W=\operatorname{span}(S)$, then "$W$ consists of all possible [[def-linear-combination_202510020342|linear combinations]] of the vectors in $S$".

This means, a linear combination like $\mathbf{w}_{1}+3\mathbf{w}_{2}-4\mathbf{w}_{3}$ is in $W$ (Assuming $r=3$).
