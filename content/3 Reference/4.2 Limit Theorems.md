## 4.2.1 Definition: f bounded on neighborhood of c

> Let 
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f:A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $c\in \mathbb{R}$ [cluster point](4.1%20Limits%20of%20Function.md#4.1.1%20Definition%20Cluster%20point) of $A$.
>
> If 
> - $\exists V_{\delta}(c),\exists M>0 \ni |f(x)| \leq M,\forall x\in A\cap V_{\delta}(c)$.
>
> Then 
> - We say **$f$ is bounded on a neigborhood of $c$**

> [!NOTE] Recall
>  The if block in the definition above is the definition of [bounded function](2.4%20Applications%20of%20the%20Supremum%20Property.md#Definition%20Bounded%20function).

## 4.2.3 Definition: Function operations

> Let 
> - $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ 
> - $f$ and $g$ be functions defined on $A$ to $\mathbb{R}$. 
>
> Then
> 
> 1. We define the **sum $f+g$**, the **difference $f-g$**, and the **product $f g$** on $A$ to $\mathbb{R}$ to be the functions given by
> 	$$
> 	\begin{align}
> 	(f+g)(x) &:= f(x) + g(x) \\
> 	(f-g)(x) &:= f(x) - g(x) \\
> 	(fg)(x) &:= f(x)g(x)
> 	\end{align}
> 	$$
> 	for all $x \in A$.
> 
> 2. If $b \in \mathbb{R}$
> 	we define the **multiple $bf$** to be the function given by
> 	$$(bf)(x) := bf(x)$$
> 	for all $x \in A$.
> 
> 3. If $h(x) \neq 0$ for $x \in A$,
>	we define the **quotient $f/h$** to be the function given by
> 	$$ \left(\frac{f}{h}\right)(x) := \frac{f(x)}{h(x)} $$ 
> 	for all $x \in A$.

## 4.2.2 Theorem

> If 
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$ 
> - $f:A\to \mathbb{R}$ has a limit at $c\in \mathbb{R}$
>
> Then **$f$ is bounded on some neighborhood of $c$**.

## 4.2.4 Theorem: Algebraic operations on limits

> Let 
> - $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f$ and $g$ be functions on $A$ to $\mathbb{R}$
> - $c \in \mathbb{R}$ cluster point of $A$
> - $b \in \mathbb{R}$
> 
> Then
> 
> 1. > If 
> 	 > - $\lim_{x \to c} f = L$  
> 	 > - $\lim_{x \to c} g = M$
> 	 > 
> 	 > Then
> 	 > $$
> 	 > \begin{align}
> 	 > \lim_{x \to c}(f+g) &= L + M \\
> 	 > \lim_{x \to c}(f-g) &= L - M \\
> 	 > \lim_{x \to c}(fg) &= LM \\
> 	 > \lim_{x \to c}(bf) &= bL \\
> 	 > \end{align}
> 	 > $$
>
> 2. > If 
> 	 > - $h: A \to \mathbb{R}$
> 	 > - $h(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \in A$
> 	 > - $\lim_{x \to c} h = H \neq 0$
> 	 > 
> 	 > Then
> 	 > $$ \lim_{x \to c}\left(\frac{f}{h}\right) = \frac{L}{H} $$

## 4.2.6 Theorem: Limit between two constants

> Let
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f:A\to R$
> - $c\in \mathbb{R}$ cluster point of $A$
> 
> If
> 
> - $a\leq f(x) \leq b \quad \forall x\in A, x\neq c$
> - $\lim_{ x \to c }f$  exists
> 
> Then
> $$ a\leq \lim_{ x \to c } f \leq b $$

## 4.2.7 Theorem: Squeeze theorem

> Let
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f,g,h:A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $c\in \mathbb{R}$ cluster point of $A$
> 
> If
> - $f(x)\leq g(x) \leq h(x) \quad \forall x\in A, x\neq c$
> - $\lim_{ x \to c }f = L = \lim_{ x \to c }h$
> 
> Then
> $$ \lim_{ x \to c } g =L $$

## 4.2.9 Theorem: Sign preservation

> Let
> - $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> - $f:A\to \mathbb{R}$
> - $c\in \mathbb{R}$ cluster point of $A$
> 
> If
> $$
> \lim_{ x \to c } f > 0 \quad \left[\text{respectively}, \lim_{ x \to c } <0 \right]
> $$
> 
> Then 
> - $\exists V_{\delta}(c)\ni f(x)>0,\forall x\in A\cap V_{\delta}(c), x\neq c\left[\text{respectively}, f(x) < 0 \right]$

> [!NOTE]
> Basically if $f$ has a limit at $c$ that is positive (or negative), 
> then there exists a neighborhood around $c$, excluding $c$ itself
> where the function values remain positive (or negative).
