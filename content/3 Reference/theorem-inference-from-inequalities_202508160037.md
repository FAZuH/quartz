## Theorem

If $ab > 0$, then either
1. $a > 0$ and $b > 0$, or
2. $a < 0$ and $b < 0$.

## Corollary

If $ab < 0$, then either
1. $a < 0$ and $b > 0$, or
2. $a > 0$ and $b < 0$.

## Proof

Suppose the contrary is true that $a > 0$.

Choose an arbitrary $\varepsilon > 0$, for example $\varepsilon = \frac 1 2 a$. Then we have $a > \varepsilon > 0$.

Becuase the hypothesis $\varepsilon > a > 0$ (see the initial premise) leads the contradictory conclusion that $a > \varepsilon$, we can conclude that the hypothesis is incorrect and we conclude that $a = 0$.
