## Definition

Let:

- $P$ : The original proposition under discussion
- $Q$ : A related but different proposition

Then an argument commits the **Diversion (Red Herring)** fallacy if and only if it asserts:

$$
P \text{ is related to } Q \text{ and I have an argument regarding } Q \implies P \text{ is true}
$$

> [!NOTE]
> This fallacy shifts attention away from the original issue to a different but related topic.

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Politician's Defense
> **Scenario:** When asked about a budget deficit, a politician responds by talking about their opponent's personal scandals instead of addressing the financial question.
>
> **Why it fails:** The personal scandals, even if true, are irrelevant to the budget issue. The diversion avoids answering the actual question while appearing to respond.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: The Environmental Debate
> **Scenario:** In a discussion about climate policy, someone brings up that "scientists used to believe in global cooling" to shift focus from current evidence.
>
> **Why it fails:** Historical scientific consensus, even if accurately characterized, doesn't address the current state of climate science and evidence. It's a distraction from the actual data.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: The Employee's Excuse
> **Scenario:** When confronted about missing a deadline, an employee starts talking about how overworked they are and how the company doesn't appreciate employees.
>
> **Why it fails:** While workload issues might be legitimate concerns, they're being used to avoid addressing the specific failure to meet the deadline. These are separate issues.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: The Character Attack
> **Scenario:** When someone points out flaws in a celebrity's argument, defenders respond "but they donate to charity" as if this refutes the criticism.
>
> **Why it fails:** Charitable giving is admirable but irrelevant to whether their argument is logically sound. Good people can make bad arguments.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: The What About-ism
> **Scenario:** When Country A is criticized for human rights violations, its defenders respond "What about Country B's violations?" to deflect criticism.
>
> **Why it fails:** Other countries' violations don't justify or negate Country A's actions. Each issue should be evaluated on its own merits, not relativized away.
