## Definition

Let:
- $P$ : A proposition

Then an argument commits the **Appeal to Emotion** if and only if it asserts:
$$
P \text{ is associated with a positive emotional response} \implies P \text{ is true}
$$

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: Charity Advertisements
> **Scenario:** A charity shows images of sad, malnourished children while asking for donations, making viewers feel guilty if they don't contribute.
>
> **Why it fails:** While the cause may be worthy, the argument relies on manipulating emotions (guilt, pity) rather than presenting evidence about how effectively the charity uses donations.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: Political Fear-Mongering
> **Scenario:** A political ad shows frightening imagery and warns that electing the opponent will lead to disaster, without providing policy details.
>
> **Why it fails:** The argument uses fear to bypass rational evaluation of actual policy positions. Whether the opponent's policies are good or bad requires evidence, not just scary music and imagery.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: Nostalgia Marketing
> **Scenario:** An advertisement claims a product is superior because it represents "the good old days" and "traditional values."
>
> **Why it fails:** The warm feelings associated with nostalgia don't make the product objectively better. Past times weren't necessarily better, and tradition alone doesn't indicate quality.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: Patriotic Appeals
> **Scenario:** An argument that a policy must be correct because "real patriots support it" and opposing it means "hating your country."
>
> **Why it fails:** The emotional appeal to patriotism distracts from evaluating the actual merits of the policy. Good policies should stand on evidence, not patriotic fervor.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: Cute Animal Campaigns
> **Scenario:** An environmental campaign uses adorable pictures of pandas or polar bears to argue for conservation measures, focusing entirely on the animals' cuteness.
>
> **Why it fails:** While the animals are cute, conservation decisions should be based on ecological impact, biodiversity value, and ecosystem roles—not just how adorable the animals are.
