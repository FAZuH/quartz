## Definition

Let:
- $P$ : A proposition
- $S$ : The person or group claiming $P$

Then an argument commits the **Personal Attack** (Ad Hominem) if and only if it asserts:
$$
\text{I have a problem with } S \implies P \text{ is false}
$$

## Examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 1: The Hypocrite Attack
> **Scenario:** A parent advises their child not to smoke, citing health risks. The child responds, "You smoked when you were young, so your argument is invalid."
>
> **Why it fails:** The parent's past smoking doesn't change the health risks of smoking. The advice is sound regardless of whether the parent followed it themselves.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 2: The Appearance Critique
> **Scenario:** In a debate about climate policy, one politician mocks another's clothing or hairstyle instead of addressing their environmental proposals.
>
> **Why it fails:** Personal appearance has no bearing on the validity of environmental arguments. This distracts from evaluating actual policy positions.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 3: The Criminal's Testimony
> **Scenario:** A witness provides evidence in a trial. The defense attorney argues the evidence should be ignored because the witness has a criminal record.
>
> **Why it fails:** While credibility matters, a person's criminal history doesn't automatically falsify their testimony. The evidence should be evaluated on its own merits.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 4: The Source Attacking
> **Scenario:** Someone dismisses a scientific study by pointing out the researcher graduated from a lesser-known university.
>
> **Why it fails:** The quality of research depends on methodology, data, and analysis—not the prestige of the researcher's alma mater. Elite institutions produce flawed research too.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example 5: The Angry Activist
> **Scenario:** When someone presents evidence of social injustice, critics respond by calling them "angry" or "divisive" rather than addressing the evidence.
>
> **Why it fails:** The speaker's emotional state or tone doesn't determine whether their factual claims are accurate. Anger at injustice is understandable and doesn't falsify evidence.
